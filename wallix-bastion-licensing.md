# What Happens When Privileged Access Gets Out of Control?

How many administrators can access your critical servers right now and how much of what they do can actually be traced?

![a](https://idency.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/WALLIX-Bastion-Product-Image-480x480-1.png)

Privileged accounts are among the most sensitive entry points in any IT environment. A stolen administrator credential, an unmanaged SSH connection, or an unrecorded RDP session can turn a small security issue into a major incident. This is where **WALLIX Bastion** takes a different approach: instead of letting privileged users connect directly to critical systems, it places a controlled security layer between the user and the target.

## How WALLIX Bastion Works

Bastion operates as a **Privileged Access Management (PAM)** gateway. Administrators authenticate to the platform first, and access to servers, databases, network devices, or other critical resources is then granted according to predefined policies.

The process is straightforward:

1. The user authenticates to Bastion.
2. Access is evaluated against security policies.
3. Bastion establishes the connection using protocols such as SSH or RDP.
4. Credentials can remain hidden from the user.
5. Sessions can be monitored and recorded.
6. Activity and logs remain available for auditing and investigation.

This model changes privileged access from an invisible activity into a controlled and traceable process.

## Why Licensing Matters

The [WALLIX Bastion](https://golicense.net/product-category/security-license/wallix/bastion/) License is not simply a key that unlocks software features. Licensing needs to match the way privileged access is actually used across the organization.

Factors such as the number of users, concurrent sessions, session recording, deployment architecture, and subscription period can influence the required licensing scope. A mismatch can create unnecessary costs or worse, leave operational requirements uncovered.

For organizations operating across multiple sites, regulated environments, or isolated networks, licensing and deployment requirements may become more complex. Bastion also supports scenarios where activation must be performed without direct internet connectivity through signed license files.

## Where It Fits Best

WALLIX Bastion is particularly useful when organizations need stronger control over administrative access, including:

* Enterprise and multi-site infrastructures
* Financial, healthcare, and government environments
* Critical servers and network devices
* Third-party or contractor access
* Air-gapped and restricted networks
* Environments requiring detailed audit trails

The real value is visibility. Instead of asking *“Who had access?”*, security teams can determine **who connected, when they connected, what system they accessed, and what happened during the session**.
